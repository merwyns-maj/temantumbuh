## 1. ASIP tahan berapa lama (CDC)
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding/breast-milk-preparation-and-storage/handling-breastmilk.html

**Freshly expressed milk**
- Room temperature (25°C or cooler): up to 4 hours.
  - Indonesian rooms are often warmer than 25°C. Say "suhu ruang, maksimal 25°C". Never write "maksimal" in copy (lint rule): use "paling lama 4 jam" and "suhu ruang yang sejuk".
- Fridge: up to 4 days.
- Freezer: about 6 months is best; up to 12 months is acceptable.
- Cooler bag with frozen ice packs: up to 24 hours (transport).

**Thawed milk**
- Thawed in the fridge: use within 24 hours of being fully thawed.
- Brought to room temperature or warmed: use within 2 hours.
- Never refreeze.

**Leftover milk after a feed:** use within 2 hours, then throw it away.

**Copy notes:** no bottles in the art. Show storage bags, a cup, a cooler bag, a fridge and a freezer.

## 2. Demam: kapan ke dokter (AAP healthychildren.org)
Source: https://healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/fever/Pages/When-to-Call-the-Pediatrician.aspx

**Call right away**
- Under 3 months (12 weeks) with 38.0°C or higher.
- At any age, when the fever keeps going above 40°C.
- At any age with a fever and any of these signs:
  - looks very ill, unusually drowsy or very fussy
  - stiff neck
  - trouble breathing
  - unexplained rash
  - repeated vomiting or diarrhoea
  - a seizure
  - was in a very hot place, such as an overheated car

**Also call if**
- The child still acts sick after the fever comes down, or seems to be getting worse.
- The fever lasts more than 24 hours in a child under 2.
- The fever lasts more than 3 days in a child 2 or older.

**Copy notes**
- The child's behaviour matters more than the number. Keep the disclaimer.
- No medicine doses on the slides (child-dosing rule). Write "ikuti dosis di label / tanya dokter".
- No IDAI page with these thresholds turned up in search. Cite AAP. Check before posting whether there's an IDAI or Kemenkes page to add as a local source.

## 3. Urutan gigi susu (ADA MouthHealthy eruption chart)
Source: https://www.mouthhealthy.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/mouthhealthy/files/activity-sheets/adaprimarytoothdev_eng.pdf

| Tooth | Lower jaw | Upper jaw |
|---|---|---|
| Central incisor | 6-10 months (usually the first) | 8-12 months |
| Lateral incisor | 10-16 months | 9-13 months |
| First molar | 14-18 months | 13-19 months |
| Canine | 17-23 months | 16-22 months |
| Second molar | 23-31 months | 25-33 months |

**Copy notes**
- Molars come before canines. That's the surprise and the hook.
- All 20 teeth are usually in by about 33 months.
- Ranges vary. Late but within the range is normal. Gigi pertama (earlier post): no tooth by 12 months → check at the dentist or doctor. Re-check that line against the earlier post's sources before reusing it.
